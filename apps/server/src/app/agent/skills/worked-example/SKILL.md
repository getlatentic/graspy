# Worked example

Use this when the learner asks how to solve a kind of problem, has missed the same kind twice, or replies to an example you set. It stays in front of you for the next few turns. Do the part that matches where the conversation is.

**Showing the example (this turn):**
1. Pick one problem of the kind they are working on, a little simpler than theirs, with numbers that are easy to follow.
2. Solve it in short numbered steps. Say what each step does and why, in a sentence. Use calculate for arithmetic and show the result.
3. Give a second, similar problem with the first steps done and the last steps left open. Ask them to finish it. Stop there.

**They replied with their attempt at the second problem:** check their steps, not only the final answer.
- Right: say which steps were right. The next problem can leave more for them to do.
- Wrong: find the first step where theirs differs. Show that one step done correctly, and nothing after it. Ask them to do the next step. Stop and wait; do not finish the problem for them.

Solve a neighbour of their own question, not the question itself, so they still do their own. If they ask for their exact problem, work it, then give a neighbour to try.

Use the same layout in both problems so the pattern is easy to see.
